# Backups, and getting your data out

---

## Take one before you change anything

Backups cover your files, your mail and your databases. Take one by hand before any change you would
mind undoing, which mostly means before installing something, before a big upload and before deleting
anything.

Ask your hosting provider what the automatic schedule is and whether the copies leave the machine.
Both are fair questions and a good provider answers them plainly.

## What a restore does

It puts back what was in the backup, and it shows you a list of exactly what came back. If the list is
shorter than you expected, that is the useful moment to find out, which is why it is shown.

Restoring writes over what is there now, so it asks you to confirm by typing.

## Getting everything out

Your whole account leaves as a single ZIP file. You ask for it, you choose a passphrase, and the
passwords and keys inside the package are encrypted with it.

Two things about that.

**Nobody needs to approve your leaving**, and there is no process to go through. It is a button.

**Do not lose the passphrase.** Nothing decrypts the credentials inside without it, and there is no
copy of it held anywhere.

## What is in the export

Your sites and their files, your databases, your mail, and the settings that go with them. The package
lists its own contents, so you can read what you have before you rely on it.

## Somewhere else to keep a copy

A backup that only exists on the same server as the thing it is backing up is not really a backup.
Download one occasionally, or ask your provider to send them somewhere else on your behalf. The best
time to discover you cannot restore is any time other than the day you need to.
