# Taking on resellers

A reseller is a customer who sells hosting of their own on your machine. The panel supports it
properly, and this is what it means for you.

---

## What changes when you promote an account

An account becomes a reseller and can then define packages out of what you gave it, take on customers
of its own, and act on those customers' resources. Its customers sit under it and under nobody else.

What does not change is the ceiling. A reseller can only sell what you gave it. Handing a customer
more than the reseller holds is refused with both numbers in the message.

## Deciding what to give one

Give a package that is the whole of what they may resell, not a starting point you plan to raise
weekly. Raising it is easy, and the number they hold is also the number your machine has to be able
to serve, so it is worth being deliberate about the total across every reseller you take on.

## What you can still see

Everything. It is your machine, and the record covers every action on it, including theirs and their
customers'. That is worth saying out loud to a reseller when they sign up, because the honest version
of this arrangement is that they are running a business on your box, not that they have a private
one.

## What they cannot reach

The firewall, the services, the machine's updates, the panel's own settings, and any other reseller's
customers. The isolation is enforced on ownership records rather than by filtering screens, and it
was attacked on purpose on a live machine: twenty-three forbidden paths, twenty-three refusals, and
nothing belonging to the other reseller moved.

## When a reseller leaves

Their customers are accounts on your machine, and each one exports as a ZIP the same as any other
account. Decide before it happens whether a departing reseller takes their customers with them or
whether the customers become yours, because that is a commercial question and the panel will do
either.
